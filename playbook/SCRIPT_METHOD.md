# Script method — write it like a copywriter who's made product ads for twenty years

Nothing gets designed until this page's output (`script.json`) passes the gate. Sources and platform guidance are listed
at the bottom.

## 1 · Research (30 minutes, minimum)
Read the product page top to bottom, the reviews, the brand's own ads and captions. Write down, in their words:
- **What it is** (category, format, size, price).
- **What it does** — the functional benefit (hydrates for 100 h; lines and contours the lip; smells like lava cake).
- **What it means** — the emotional benefit (I feel put-together; I smell like a holiday; I don't have to try hard).
- **Proof** — numbers, ingredients, notes, awards, the visible result.
- **The audience** — one person, not a demographic: "a 24-year-old in Mumbai who wears three lip products to brunch".
- **The tension** — what's annoying, funny or true about their life that this product answers.

## 2 · Insight → SMP
- **Insight:** a true, slightly uncomfortable observation about the audience. "Everyone owns five lip balms and still
  buys the next one." "Dessert-sweet perfumes smell like the thing you're trying not to eat."
- **SMP (single-minded proposition):** the ONE thing the reel must make someone feel or believe. ≤ 14 words, one
  sentence. If you can't write it in one sentence, you have two ads.
  - good: "You can't eat this, but you can wear it." · "One dot is enough." · "Smell like Rio in 1962."
  - bad: "Three new launches for every skin need." · "Mood-based fragrances with unique notes for every occasion."

## 3 · Pick the story shape (one)
| Shape | Use when | Skeleton (≈ 20–30 s) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tension → release** | there's a relatable problem | hook names the problem (0–3 s) → it gets worse/funny → product enters → proof → payoff line → CTA |
| **Demo / proof** | the product does something visible | hook = the result or the weirdest moment of the demo → how → proof close-up → payoff → CTA |
| **Before / after** | the change is the point | hook = the after (promise first) → before → the routine → after again, held → CTA |
| **Reframe / pun** | the product has a built-in joke (names, numbers) | hook = the joke's setup → the turn → the joke paid off THROUGH the product's benefit → CTA |
| **Desire / sensorial** | fragrance, texture, food-adjacent | hook = the most sensual close-up → what it smells/feels like (one metaphor, held) → wear it → CTA |
| **POV / meme** | the audience already jokes about it | hook = the meme format → the product as the punchline → proof → CTA |

A pun or a device is a **delivery** for the SMP, never the SMP. Test: remove the device; does the reel still sell the
product? If not, the device is decoration (Lesson L03).

## 4 · Beat sheet (≤ 6 beats)
Each beat has a role (`hook`, `turn`, `reveal`, `proof`, `payoff`, `cta`, optionally `montage`), a time, and three
columns — this is where the Kylie reel failed, so the columns are mandatory:

| says (the super or VO, ≤ 7 words) | shows (what the footage literally shows) | serves (how it moves the SMP forward) |
|---|---|---|
| "you can't eat this…" | chocolate pouring over the bottle | sets the indulgence |
| "…but you can wear it." | the mist sprayed on a neck | turns food into fragrance |

Rules: the shot must SHOW what the line SAYS (a fragrance line gets spraying, wrists, the bottle, the notes as food —
never a blush brush). One line per beat. The product is on screen by 3 s. One proof beat. The last beat names the
product, what it is, the price or where.

## 5 · Write the supers like a headline writer
- Short words, present tense, one thought per super, ≤ 7 words. Read it aloud on the beat: if it doesn't fit, cut.
- Concrete beats abstract: "lasts through a 14-hour shift" beats "long-lasting".
- Use the audience's words (reviews, comments), not the brand's internal names, except for the product name.
- A twist line needs its setup on screen long enough to read (≥ 0.3 s per word + 0.5 s).
- Hinglish/Hindi supers for Indian brands when the audience speaks that way; keep the product name in the brand's form.

## 6 · Music is part of the script
Name the song in the script, with where its hook and drop fall (seconds). The edit's turn or reveal sits on the drop.
See `MUSIC.md`.

## 7 · The stranger test (before design, and again on the draft)
Read only the `says` column, then only the `shows` column. Each alone must tell the same story. Then answer, as a
stranger: **what is it? what does it do for me? why should I care?** Those three answers go into `recall` and must
appear on screen.

## Worked example — how the Kylie reel should have been written
- Insight: you change outfits three times a day but wear the same perfume.
- SMP: "Change your scent like you change your fit." (8 words)
- Shape: desire / sensorial with a time-of-day spine.
- Beats: hook (0–3 s) Kylie sprays, freeze, "8 am you ≠ 11 pm you." → three time stamps, each = the bottle sprayed in a
  matching setting + its notes as food/objects (no makeup shots) → proof: the three bottles side by side, "three eau de
  parfums" → CTA "Mood Stones EDP · kyliecosmetics.com".
- Music: a late-night R&B or pop song with a drop at the 11 pm switch.
